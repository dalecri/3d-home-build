# Repeating this for a client: what to ask for

What slowed this build down, and what would have removed the guesswork.

## Must have
1. **A floor plan, even a hand sketch with rough measurements.** This was the biggest unknown. Without
   one, room positions had to be worked out by cross-referencing which doors, windows and fixtures show
   up in which photos. A phone sketch with arrows ("kitchen window looks onto the porch") is enough.
2. **One exterior anchor measurement.** Here it was the 12.5 x 6.0 m footprint from aerial imagery. A
   municipal assessment roll, a certificate of location (certificat de localisation) or a tape-measured
   wall all work.
3. **Photos of every room from 2 opposite corners**, landscape, at chest height, lights on, doors open.
   Listing photos are shot to flatter, so they skip closets, bathrooms and hallways, and they never show
   the wall the photographer stands against.
4. **A list of rooms**, including the ones nobody photographs (bathroom, laundry, mudroom).
5. **The lot**: certificate of location or a cadastre screenshot, plus an aerial (Google Earth) with
   north marked.

## Nice to have
- **A walkthrough video** (one slow pass through the house). It shows how rooms connect, which photos
  don't. Even 60 seconds on a phone helps.
- **Ceiling height and one door width** for interior scale.
- **Close-ups of finishes**: floor, wainscot, countertop, fireplace stone. These become the textures.
- **Exterior photos as files**, not pasted into a chat, so the critic can compare against them directly.
- **What the model is for** (selling, renovating, furnishing, nostalgia). That decides whether furniture
  is matched exactly or just staged.

## Process that worked
- Fix the floor plan first, with a camera position for every photo. Everything else is judged from those
  positions.
- One builder per room, plus a critic with fresh context who only sees our render next to the real
  photo and names the single biggest gap. Fresh eyes catch things the builder stops noticing (wrong
  camera height, a door swung into the shot, wainscot the wrong colour).
- Ship as a single HTML file (about 1 MB, no install), with a "compare with photo" button. The side-by-side
  view is what sells it.

## Honest limits
- Areas that weren't photographed are educated guesses and should be labelled that way.
- Procedural textures and simple furniture models read as "recognisably your house" rather than photoreal.
  Photoreal would need scanned assets (Polycam/LiDAR on a recent iPhone) or a lot more modelling time per
  room.
- Listing photos use wide lenses that distort proportions. A floor plan with measurements beats
  reverse-engineering from photos every time.
